<Lede>
A list of "engineering leaders at Series A companies" is a list of people who share a job title. It says nothing about whether any of them has the problem you solve this month.
</Lede>

At Shiplight we sell to teams adopting AI coding tools and looking for a way to test what those tools produce. That is not a title. It is a behavior, and behavior leaves traces.

## What we looked for

Spec-driven development was the tell. Teams that write specifications for agents to implement are the teams that end up with a lot of generated code and a testing problem. So I wrote a Python collector that reads public GitHub activity around that pattern: issues, pull requests, forks, and actual spec files in repositories. It hydrates the profiles, normalizes company domains, dedupes, caches raw responses, and respects the rate limits.

The output was roughly 5,300 signal rows describing 4,500 unique people and nearly 600 organizations.

## What a signal is not

This is where most "intent data" goes wrong. A star, a fork, or a comment is not buying intent. It is a reason to look closer. So the raw collector output never became the outreach list. It became the research queue.

From there:

1. **Enrich.** Who is this person, what does the company do, how big is it, does it plausibly ship software the way we help with.
2. **Qualify.** Does the behavior match the problem, or is it noise. A student experimenting with a spec template is not a buyer. A platform team adding spec files across twenty repositories might be.
3. **Personalize from the evidence.** The outreach references what they are doing, not what their title suggests they might care about.

## Why it worked better

Two reasons. First, the conversation opens with something true about the recipient, which is rare enough to earn a reply. Second, the list is self-refreshing. Titles change once a year. Behavior changes every week, and the collector runs on a schedule.

## Keeping it honest

I keep three boundaries in front of me:

- Signals are research inputs. The conversion rate of a signal is not established until you measure it.
- A big CSV is not an asset. Enrichment quality matters more than row count.
- Do not multiply a list by an imagined deal size. That number will end up in a deck and someone will believe it.

The best thing about sourcing from behavior is that it forces you to actually understand what the person is doing. That understanding is the product. The list is a byproduct.
