<Lede>
Shiplight builds AI agents that write and run end-to-end software tests. I joined in June 2026 as the first go-to-market hire, reporting to the founders, with a mandate that was one sentence long: find the people who need this, and make it easy for them to say yes.
</Lede>

## The situation

A technical product, a technical buyer, two founders who were still doing every sales conversation themselves, and a self-serve launch on the calendar. There was no pipeline system, no reliable funnel reporting, and no repeatable way to find developers who were already adopting AI coding tools.

The job was not to run a playbook. It was to build one while running it.

## What I built

### A sourcing engine based on behavior, not titles

Title-based lists were producing generic outreach. I wrote a Python collector that reads public GitHub activity around spec-driven development, hydrates the profiles, and normalizes company domains. It produced research on 4,500+ developer profiles and nearly 600 organizations, which we then enriched and qualified before anyone was contacted.

I treat those signals as research inputs, not intent. A repository action does not mean someone is buying. It means they are worth understanding.

### Outbound that people actually answered

I ran persona-based LinkedIn outreach through my own sender account: 2,300+ contacts reached, nearly 400 message conversations started, and a 35% reply rate as reported by the platform. The interesting work was not the sequence. It was reading each reply and deciding whether to ask a discovery question, follow up, or suggest a call, and writing that judgment down as reusable guidance so the founders' voice stayed consistent.

### Funnel reporting the founders could trust

When I audited the analytics, a "lead" event was firing on contact-page views. Sixty-eight supposed leads were page views. Signup queries silently capped at 100 rows. "Activation" meant answering a short survey.

I rebuilt the reporting across GA4, PostHog, and Google Search Console, redefined activation as a real test run by the signup account, and reconciled registrations into external signups and activated accounts. The result was a number the team could reason about, which mattered more than a bigger one.

### Search and AI discovery infrastructure

I owned technical SEO and AI-search discoverability for the marketing site: structured data, an `llms.txt` that actually linked to things, and a recurring indexing monitor with history, notifications, and catch-up after downtime. The first honest run exposed 62 unindexed URLs out of 240. Later runs reached full coverage of the monitored inventory.

Alongside targeted backlink campaigns and editorial partnerships the team ran, referring domains grew from 148 to 482 and Ahrefs Domain Rating rose from 5 to 30 between June and September.

### Launch, content, and video

For the August self-serve launch, the founder asked me to drive execution while he was unavailable: launch video, warm-contact emails, social timing, partner demos, and launch-day communications. I iterated the launch video to a final cut, then built a reusable video production workflow in Python and React/Remotion covering scripts, animation, narration, captions, and publication checks. Two educational videos and a repaired Dev.to channel followed.

## How I worked

I used coding agents heavily. My job was choosing the problem, supplying the customer and product context, directing the implementation, and validating the result. Fifty-four merged pull requests across three repositories is the paper trail, but the point is that a go-to-market person no longer has to wait for engineering to fix the funnel.

<Callout>
Numbers here follow the boundaries in my own records. Reply rates are platform-reported and include partnership outreach. Search and referral trends are team results during the period, not a causal measure of my work alone.
</Callout>

## What I took from it

Growth is a systems problem before it is a copywriting problem. The fastest wins came from making things measurable, then trusting the measurement enough to change direction.
