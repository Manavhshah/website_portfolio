<Lede>
The challenge asked a simple question: what will the world look like as nations use debt, data, and industrial strategy to secure power in a post-globalization economy? I answered with a system rather than an essay.
</Lede>

## What I built

- A repeatable forecasting template linking each prediction to a probability, a timeframe, and an explicit cause-and-effect chain, so it could be scored later.
- Twenty predictions across education, AI, energy, fiscal dynamics, and currency competition.
- A synthesizing thesis on "competitive sovereignty": the shift from open markets toward state-led advantage, and what it does to trade and monetary policy.

## Method

Bridgewater-style probabilistic reasoning with a bias toward falsifiability. If a prediction could not be wrong, it did not make the list. The output doubled as a calibration baseline I can check my own judgment against over time.

## Why it is here

It is the clearest example of how I like to think: build the framework first, then let the framework produce the opinions.
