<Lede>
Students, founders, and early-career operators juggle a few hundred active relationships at once, spread across texts, email, LinkedIn, and rooms. Nobody keeps up. Alligator was my answer: log an interaction in plain language, and let the system remember the names, topics, and open threads for you.
</Lede>

## The problem

CRMs are built for pipelines. They demand structured entry and reward nobody who is not paid to fill them in. So people rely on memory, and memory loses. Details are forgotten, follow-ups slip, and the network someone spent years building goes under-used.

## What we built

A relationship-memory system. You write what happened the way you would tell a friend. Alligator extracts people, topics, and commitments, builds living profiles, and lets you ask questions in natural language before your next conversation: what did we talk about, what did I promise, what changed.

Short term it was a meeting-prep tool. The long-term vision was a social copilot that surfaces reconnection opportunities on its own.

## My role

I founded it and led the commercial side: problem framing, the semester validation plan, structured customer interviews, and the beta cohort, working with three engineers on the MVP. We entered it into the Cozad New Venture Challenge, UIUC's flagship student venture competition.

## Where it stands

Paused. The product taught me more about behavior than about AI. Extraction was the easy part. Getting people to log consistently was the whole game, and I was not yet convinced we had found the wedge that changes habits. I would rather stop with the lesson than scale without it.
